## Create a Haiku

<CustomAside type="excerpt" reference="Exercise 6.1.1 (p.197)"></CustomAside>

We suggest you read these instructions and then leave your digital
devices and go outside. The weather might be nice, or not, but hopefully you can go
somewhere where you can sit and experience the moment. Unplugging from the inter-
net and the work of computers is good for your physical and mental health, and we
hope you will find time to reflect and find something beautiful in your environment.

The haiku is a Japanese poetry form dating back to the seventeenth century. Traditional
haikus include three elements: a word that references the natural world or seasons (kigo),
a word that implies you should stop and be reflective (kareji), and sensory imagery.17 Your
haiku will help you to generate imagery and feeling (or mood) for your project. The haiku
format adheres to three non-rhyming lines, with five syllables in the first line, seven syl-
lables on the next line, followed by five syllables on the last line

1. Go outside and bring only a pen or pencil and paper. Read the next two steps, then
start with either the haiku or drawing, as described in the next two steps.
2. Write a haiku about your experience using language that inspires imagery in the
5-7-5 syllable count on three sequential lines. You can employ one of the OuLiPo
strategies mentioned in section 6.1.1 if you feel stuck.
3. Create a drawing or series of drawings inspired by your haiku (or, if you are drawing first,
write a haiku inspired by your drawings). It doesn't need to be a work of “Art” but rather
forms that express the imagery, observations, or mood you felt. You will eventually use
the text and sketches to create a design that expresses the sentiment in the poem.
