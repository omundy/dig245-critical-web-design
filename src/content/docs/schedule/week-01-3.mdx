### Learning objectives

{/* By the end of this session you will be able to: */}

1. Map the power relationships and economic flows of a digital platform (ownership, payment, profit, exclusion).
2. Apply vocabulary from Ch 1 Context to describe the political economy of a specific web infrastructure.
3. Articulate a critical position on how network infrastructure shapes who has power on the web.




## 👉 Activity — Where Does It Actually Go? (15 min)

Open [geotraceroute.com](https://geotraceroute.com) a browser-based traceroute that maps each network hop geographically.

Run a trace on 3 sites and note: how many hops, which cities/countries the data passes through, and which companies own those hops (look at the hostnames `ae-3.r01.chrlncus.qwest.net` tells you a lot).

Suggested targets to compare:

- A major US site ([google.com](https://google.com), [nytimes.com](https://nytimes.com))
- A site from another country ([bbc.co.uk](https://bbc.co.uk), [theguardian.com](https://theguardian.com))
- A site you actually use daily

Discuss (last 5 min): What surprised you? The phrase "the cloud" implies data lives somewhere abstract — where does it actually live? Who owns the cables between those dots on the map? See [submarinecablemap.com](https://www.submarinecablemap.com/)
